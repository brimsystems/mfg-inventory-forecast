Section 1

Executive Summary

From January through June 2026 the shop ran its purchasing on the cleaned records and on the model's reorder points and order quantities, and it carried less stock while running short less often. Inventory fell from an average of $3.48M a month in 2025 under manual reordering to $3.01M in June 2026, 13% lower. Part of that decline would have happened anyway: replaying 2026 with manual reordering continued leaves June at $3.15M, so the model's own effect is the 5% gap between the two Junes. Against the first half of 2025, run the way the shop had always run it, stockout events fell from 374 to 250, jobs held for material from 75 to 65, and rush freight and premiums from $142K to $98K.

Inventory (2025 average month against June 2026); stockouts, held jobs and rush spend by half-year

The manual process held more stock than the shop needed, in the wrong places. Reorder points set at go-live and never revisited were far too high on some items and too low on others, and buyers bought about four and a half months of an item at a time whatever it cost. The model moves the stock to where it prevents a stoppage. Each item's buffer is sized to how far off its own forecasts and deliveries have been, jobs already booked are netted weeks before they draw items, and expensive items are bought more often in smaller lots. Replaying the same six months of demand with nothing fixed shows the gain once the new policy has settled (months 4 to 6): stockout events fall 49%, jobs held for material 74% and rush spend 53%. Inventory is still falling at the end of June, as excess on slow items is used up and not replaced.

Section 2

Model Overview

Section 2.1

What This Model Does

Over the past six months (January 2026 to June 2026), the demand forecasting model has set the shop's reorder decisions. Every week, the model predicts which items need to be reordered, and these predictions are then fed directly into the ERP for each of the 1,300 stocked items. The model's reorder decisions are based on the current stock on hand and on order for each item, the forecasted usage over each supplier's delivery time, and a safety buffer based on how unpredictable each item's demand and deliveries have been.

1. What it reads
Three years of cleaned usage history for every stocked item, with its demand pattern, value class, cost and supplier lead time.
→
2. What it predicts
Every week, one forecast per item: how many units the shop will use before a new order placed today could arrive.
→
3. What it produces
For every item, when to reorder and how much, loaded into the ERP, which checks every item daily and flags what to order.

The model refreshes its forecasts every week and is retrained on the latest history once a month. The model's predictions are loaded directly into the ERP, which flags when each item should be reordered and how much to order. To keep working capital low, the recommendations for how much to order are informed by the item's cost: expensive items are bought every few weeks in small lots, and cheap items a few times a year in larger lots.

The ERP's reorder queue ranks every stocked item against the model's weekly reorder point: items at or below it are marked "ORDER NOW", items within two weeks of it "ORDER SOON", and items more than two weeks outside of it "OK". Section 2.2

Training Data Overview

The model learns from the shop's historical usage of every stocked item. It was originally trained on three years of this usage data (2023 through 2025, as shown below), and is continually trained on every new month of data.

MONTHLY USAGE IN VALUE, BY ITEM CATEGORY (JAN. 2023 to DEC. 2025)
MONTHLY USAGE IN UNITS, BY ITEM CATEGORY (JAN. 2023 to DEC. 2025)

Demand is steady in aggregate over the three years, but individual items exhibit very different demand patterns. We've categorized these individual item demand patterns into four groups, which the model is calibrated against: 418 smooth (regular and steady), 430 erratic (regular but variable), 270 lumpy (irregular and variable) and 182 intermittent (many months with no demand at all). One representative item within each pattern is shown below.

Three years of monthly usage, one representative item per demand pattern

Individual demand patterns are informed by the kind of item, though every category carries all four patterns. Smooth demand is most common among fasteners (50%) and consumables (44%), the floor stock used consistently every week. Erratic demand is spread widely, and is highest among hardware (41%) and raw material (35%). Lumpy demand concentrates in raw material (31%) and outside service (29%). Intermittent demand is highest in mechanical (30%) and electrical (16%), where just a few of these high-cost items are used for a job or sold as occasional spares.

Items in each category, by demand pattern

Item cost and usage move in opposite directions across the categories. Mechanical and electrical items (motors, gearboxes, drives, controls) are 35% of items and 57% of usage value, with median unit costs of $41 and $25, but only 12% of the units used. Fasteners and hardware are the reverse: 53% of the units used but 9% of the value, at median unit costs under $2. The shop's working capital is therefore tied up in a few hundred expensive, slower-moving items, not in the high-volume floor stock.

Section 3

Model Performance

Section 3.1

Scoring Summary

From January to June 2026 the model refreshed the reorder points of all 1,300 items every week. Across those 26 weekly refreshes it made 33,800 predictions: 1,254 (4%) were marked ORDER NOW and 3,417 (10%) ORDER SOON, and the rest were OK. In a typical week that is about 48 items to order now and 131 to order soon, a short, ranked list for the buyers to work through.

Section 3.2

Accuracy and Validation

The model was chosen and calibrated before go-live on a held-out year of 2025, which it had not seen during training. Three candidate algorithms were tuned and compared on their forecasts for that year, and the random forest model, with the lowest error, was selected. Every other result in this section measures how the model then performed in live use, from January to June 2026, against how the shop performed before it.
