Section 1

Status & Retraining Decision

The demand forecasting model has been monitored monthly across its live window, January to June 2026. Forecast error and bias are the primary model triggers (i.e., when these exceed their Retrain thresholds, the model must be retrained), while target, prediction and feature drift are leading indicators. Business KPIs including fill rates, stockout events and jobs held are also monitored against target levels.

A forecast can only be scored once its lead-time window has closed, so each month is judged on accuracy once at least 80% of its forecasts have matured. By June 30th that covers January to April; May and June are shown but not yet judged on accuracy.

The flag reads RETRAIN RECOMMENDED, meaning the model should be fully retrained on data through June. This is because the model's forecast error on intermittent items rose above its Retrain threshold in March and April. Separately, the safety buffers should be recalibrated, because the fill rates achieved ran more than 1 point below their targets for the five months between January and May.

Section 2.2

Drift

Drift compares each month's data with a fixed reference calculated using the Jensen-Shannon distance, flagged at 0.10. In other words, it checks for statistical similarity: whether this month's values fall across the same ranges, in similar proportions, as the reference, where 0 means the two are identical and 1 means they do not overlap at all. It is checked three ways: on the targets (lead-time usage), on the model's forecasts, and on the model's features.

Target drift. Distance between each month's actual lead-time usage and the training rows. A shift would mean demand itself has moved away from what the model learned. Across the matured months target drift stays well under the threshold (0.031 to 0.045), meaning monthly demand resembles the demand the model was trained on.

Target Drift Distance by Month

Prediction drift. Distance between each month's forecasts and the forecasts from the held-out 2025 year. Prediction drift stays under the threshold every month (at most 0.081), so the model is producing forecasts on the same scale and spread as in 2025.

Section 2.4

Business KPIs

Fill rate is measured against each item type's target: 99% for production items and 98.5% for spare parts and shop supplies. Most of the time, item types fell below their targets: across the six months and three item types, fill rate was below target in 15 of 18 cases, and more than 1 point below in 10 of them. Shop supplies missed their target in every month, spare parts in 5 and production items in 4, with the largest gap for production items in April (2.6 points). By Jun 2026 the gaps had narrowed to 0.6 points or less. Overall forecast bias is small, so the shortfall points to the safety buffers: calibrated on 2025 errors, they are not quite reaching the targets, and should be recalibrated on the January to June errors. The early months also carry the handover from the manual stock levels.
